## 1. The eight interview answers, verbatim

**1. What is this, and who is it for / vibe**

> "The brand is the website for my old work, Home Ground London. They're a
> creative agency. It should showcase their previous work with videos and
> clients. It should really highlight their ability to help brands and state why
> they would be a suitable fit for working with clients while engaging audiences
> and any consumer or social media fan that looks at their website."

> "It should feel like an agency that offers high-quality attention to detail and
> a high level of output for the biggest brands in sport."

**2. Vibe in three to five words, plus references**

> "Engaging, energetic, fun... offering an edge in the market for sports creation
> and social media content." Premium.

No outside-medium references were given. The reference material used instead is
the agency's own body of work (kit-launch films, athlete films), which the user
pointed at directly.

**3. The scroll journey, section by section, in their words**

> "They should first be hit by the typography, the logo, and what Home Ground
> offers. There should be some clear video examples of creative campaigns that
> are currently there and should just be reintegrated into the new website in a
> fun and engaging manner."

> "They should be drawn to the previous campaign straight away. It should be the
> main focus point."

**4. The energy curve**

> "It should feel highly energetic. It should only feel calm around the sections
> where they're explaining what they offer to clients and what their unique
> selling point is."

**5. How should someone feel, and the ONE moment**

Answered through the quality/craft frame rather than as a list of emotions:

> "Make it really clear who they're working for, what they've produced, and the
> quality. Make sure it really resonates with quality."

The one moment is therefore engineered around the thing the user said was
broken: you cannot tell who Homeground works with. See **The peak** below.

**6. How far from premium-minimal**

> "It should feel premium."

Premium, but not quiet-premium: the user asked for energetic and fun in the same
breath. Resolved as **premium editorial-gallery**: large type, generous plates,
hard grounds, no ornament.

**7. One unbroken world, or distinct scenes?**

> "I believe it's your judgment what you think fits best for the distinct scenes
> and chapters, but it should have a clear flow while being really engaging and
> really showcasing the successful work they have done previously."

Called: **distinct scenes.** Each project is its own plate. See grammar note.

**8. What assets do they already have?**

> "I can provide access to the Instagram page and the old website so you can
> extract typography, brown colors, and the logo. The logo is sort of green with
> H and G in black writing, but I will extract this for you."

> https://www.homeground.london/ · https://www.instagram.com/homegroundldn/

**Both links are blocked by this environment's network egress proxy, and there is
no asset-generation budget available (Higgsfield: 0 credits, no free allowance;
no KIE key).** So no footage and no generated imagery was obtainable. Every media
slot on the page is an authored, correctly-proportioned placeholder that names
the campaign that belongs in it. See `SLOTS.md`.

**Mid-build the user supplied the real logo and brand colour directly** (an
arched collegiate "HOMEGROUND" wordmark, black on an acid lime), which replaced
the earlier placeholder mark and green. See §10.

## 3. Grammar: Gallery / catalog

Chosen because the second-pass direction describes a catalogue almost word for
word: objects in a walkable collection, one uniform label schema, no object
squeezed, the client legible at a glance.

Why the other seven lost:

| Grammar | Why not |
|---|---|
| Filmic one-shot | Carries a burden of proof in this skill, and fails it here. One continuous argument buries the individual projects inside a flow; the user's complaint is precisely that individual clients are not legible. |
| Rhythmic cutlist | Was the leading choice on the first-pass answers alone (sport, energy, "highly energetic"). Killed by "make sure the campaigns aren't too squished": the grammar caps every act at ~1.4vh and bans dwell, which is a machine for squishing campaigns. |
| Chaptered editorial | Right restraint, wrong unit. Chapters are prose; these objects are films with credits, and the media must not sit in a captioned column. |
| Live surface | There is no product surface to run. Homeground makes films. |
| Continuous world | No geography in the brief, and it is the most fragile build available. |
| Typographic poster | Tempting given zero assets, and it would satisfy "hit by the typography". But it forbids media entirely, and the user's number-one ask is that the campaign work is the main focus point. |
| Split stage | No two-sided argument here. |

**What the grammar gives, against what the user asked for:** the nav is an index
of objects and it jumps (→ "clients more readily available"); every object gets
the same label schema with no exceptions (→ "for each client, it should match the
project"); labels are fact, not pitch (→ "what they've produced, and the
quality"); the close is an inquiry plate typeset as a label.

**Where the brief overrides the grammar, deliberately:** the grammar says the
hero is object one with no separate title treatment. The user asked to be "hit by
the typography, the logo, and what Home Ground offers" first. Resolved without
breaking grammar: the wordmark is set at composition scale as part of object
one's own plate, and "what Homeground offers" lives as a standing head in the
chrome index rather than as a hero claim. No separate title stage was added.

**The offer/USP sections are objects too.** Rather than an argument-shaped act
(which this grammar forbids), the three capabilities are catalogued with the
identical label schema as the client work. That is where the calm sits, per
answer 4.

## 4. The feeling curve

Written before the acts. Emotion first, cause second.

```
1   Arrival        the wordmark at composition scale on a black plate, and one
                   project already named and labelled under it. Nothing spins.
2   Recognition    Beats by Dre and Cole Palmer, given a whole screen, credits
                   set like a title card. A known name, treated seriously.
3   Appetite       Manchester United and Barry Keoghan on the opposite anchor.
                   The schema repeats, so the eye starts trusting it.
4   Trust          the ground cuts to bone. Callaway, in daylight. The change of
                   ground says this collection has range, not one register.
5   Calm           the three capabilities, catalogued exactly like the work.
                   The only place on the page where nothing is being shown off.
6   Weight         Homeground Academy. The object that is not a client. Quiet,
                   and it is the silence in front of the peak.
7   AWE  (peak)    the client index leaves the margin and takes the whole
                   screen, every client and every project travelling sideways
                   at once. The thing that was a small list is suddenly the
                   scale of the business.
8   Resolve        the index collapses back. An inquiry plate typeset as a
                   label, one action, and it holds.
```

No two adjacent acts share a feeling. Act 6 is deliberately the quietest thing on
the page because act 7 is the loudest.

**Authored silence:** act 6 (Academy) is intentionally near-empty and slow. It is
not dead scroll. The verification pass should read it as the run-up to the peak.

## 5. The peak

> "the client list that had been quietly ticking along in the margin suddenly
> took over the entire screen, and I realised how many of them there were"

Lives in **act 7**. It gets the largest span on the page by a visible margin, it
gets the lateral `pan` (the grammar's spine device, spent at the biggest
moment), and act 6 is authored quiet so it has something to arrive from.

It is the peak because it answers the user's actual complaint. The clients were
invisible; the page ends by making them unmissable.

An experience, from the visitor's side. The signature move and the peak are the

## 7. The signature move

**The living client index.**

A persistent index in the page margin listing every client. It starts unlit. As
each project plate passes, that client's row lights, stamps with what was
produced, and *stays* lit for the rest of the page, so the index accumulates into
a complete, legible client list rather than being a static nav. Every row jumps
to its project, which is what the grammar requires of a gallery nav.

At the collection's end it breaks out of the margin and becomes the full-screen
client wall (the peak), then collapses back down into the inquiry plate.

Not a kit device, not a parameter change. Driven from each act's `--sc-p` and the
page's own scroll state in page-level JS. The engine is untouched.

## 10. The brand, as supplied

The user sent the real logo mid-build: an **arched collegiate/varsity
"HOMEGROUND" wordmark, near-black on an acid lime field**. That replaced the
provisional "HG in a green chip" placeholder and the earlier green.

**Colour.** One hue, three lightnesses, because the page hard-cuts between three
grounds and no single stop clears 4.5:1 on all of them:

| Token | Value | Role | Measured |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--hg-lime` | `#7CEE00` | the brand field, and the accent on black | 13.1:1 on black |
| `--hg-lime-deep` | `#3D7300` | the accent on the bone ground | 4.9:1 on bone |
| `--hg-black` | `#0B0D0B` | dark ground | — |
| `--hg-bone` | `#EEEDE7` | light ground | — |

The lime is eyeballed from the supplied image, not sampled from a file. **If you
have the hex, change `--hg-lime` in one place and the whole page follows.**

**The mark.** Set as SVG text on an arc in **Graduate** (a collegiate varsity
face, self-hosted), bulked with a painted-under stroke to match the weight of
the supplied artwork. It is real markup, so it stays sharp at any size and is
readable to screen readers. It is not a trace of the original artwork — for
exact fidelity, drop the real file at `assets/logo.svg` and swap the three
`.hg-logo` blocks. See `SLOTS.md`.

Because the hero is now the actual brand lockup, the hero plate became the lime
field with the mark at composition scale. The page therefore opens and closes on
the brand field with the work in between, which is a better answer to "they
should first be hit by the typography and the logo" than the placeholder was.

## 11. The feel check

Run per feel.md §6: scrolled cold, one word per act written down *before*
reopening this file, then diffed.

| Act | Intended | Felt (cold) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Hero | Arrival | Arrival | ✓ |
| 2 Beats by Dre | Recognition | Recognition | ✓ |
| 3 Manchester United | Appetite | Appetite | ✓ |
| 4 Callaway | Trust | Rhythm | close enough |
| 5 Sky Sports | Trust | **Repetition** | ✗ |
| 6 Aston Villa | Trust | **Repetition** | ✗ |
| 7 Arsenal | Trust | **Repetition** | ✗ |
| 8 Capabilities | Calm | Calm | ✓ |
| 9 Academy | Weight / quiet | Quiet | ✓ |
| 10 The wall | **Awe (peak)** | Interesting, not awe | ✗ |
| 11 Close | Resolve | Resolve | ✓ |

Two findings, both fixed rather than written off:

**Finding 1 — acts 5, 6 and 7 produced the same word.** feel.md says two adjacent
acts sharing a feeling means one is filler; three is worse. Cutting was not
available (the user's whole point is that every client must be visible and
matched to its project), so the fix was **pacing, not deletion**: the two
verified marquee films (Beats by Dre, Manchester United) now sit on a wider
cinematic plate at 2:1, and the rest keep the standard plate. The collection now
has hierarchy while every object keeps the identical label schema the grammar
requires. Honest caveat: a good part of what made 5–7 feel repetitive is that
their media slots are all the same empty grey. Real footage will differentiate
them further than any layout change can.

**Finding 2 — the peak did not read as the peak.** On the contact sheet the
loudest things on the page were the two lime plates; the wall was dark cards on
a dark ground, i.e. the *least* contrasty act on the page while holding the
largest span. feel.md: "If a different act is the biggest thing on the sheet,
that act is the real peak and the plan lost." Fixed by making the wall's cards
the brand field itself — seven lime plates sweeping across black. It is now
unambiguously the largest visual change on the page, and it makes the signature
move literal: the small lime rules ticking along in the margin index become
full lime plates filling the screen.

Both re-checked on a fresh contact sheet after the fix.
